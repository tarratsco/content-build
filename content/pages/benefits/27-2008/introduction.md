# Apply for a United States Burial Flag

VA provides a United States flag to drape over the casket — or accompany the
urn — of a deceased Veteran as a symbol of their service and sacrifice. After
the funeral service, the flag is given to the Veteran's next-of-kin or another
authorized person to keep.

**VA Form 27-2008** is the official application for a United States burial flag.

[Start your application](/burials-memorials/memorial-items/burial-flag/introduction){.usa-button.usa-button-primary}

[Download VA Form 27-2008 (PDF)](https://www.va.gov/find-forms/about-form-27-2008/){.action-link}

---

## Who can apply

The following people can request a burial flag for a deceased Veteran:

- **Next-of-kin** — surviving spouse, child, parent, brother, sister, or
  other close relative
- **Funeral director or funeral home representative** — submitting on behalf
  of the Veteran's family
- **Veterans Service Organization (VSO) representative** — acting as an
  authorized representative
- **Close friend of the Veteran** — when there is no living next-of-kin

Only one flag may be issued for each deceased Veteran.

---

## Who is eligible

A burial flag may be issued for a Veteran who:

- Was discharged from active military service under conditions other than
  dishonorable, **or**
- Died while on active duty

**If the Veteran served in the Selected Reserve** (Army National Guard, Air
National Guard, Army Reserve, Naval Reserve, Marine Corps Reserve, Air Force
Reserve, or Coast Guard Reserve), the Veteran must also meet **at least one**
of these criteria:

- Was entitled to retired pay for reserve service, or would have been entitled
  but was under age 60
- Served at least one full enlistment period (or, as an officer, completed the
  initial period of obligation)
- Was discharged because of a disability incurred or made worse in the line of
  duty
- Died while a member of the Selected Reserve

**Veterans of the U.S. Public Health Service (USPHS) and the National Oceanic
and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA)** may also be eligible. VA will review
your application to confirm eligibility.

**Veterans who served in the Philippine military forces** under the President's
Order of July 26, 1941, may be eligible if the Veteran died on or after
April 25, 1951.

### Discharge character

Veterans with a **dishonorable discharge** are not eligible for a burial flag.
If you are unsure of the Veteran's discharge character, you can still submit
this application. VA will make the final eligibility determination.

To request discharge records, contact the National Personnel Records Center
(NPRC) at [archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records](https://www.archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records)
or call **1-86-NARA-NARA**.

## What you'll need before you start

Gathering this information ahead of time will help you complete the application
faster. The form takes about **15 minutes** to fill out.

### About the Veteran

- Veteran's full name (as it appears on discharge documents)
- Veteran's Social Security Number (optional — helps VA locate records faster)
- VA file number (optional — found on previous VA correspondence)
- Military service number or serial number (optional — for Veterans who
  served before Social Security Numbers were used as military identifiers,
  generally before July 1, 1969)
- Branch of service
- Date entered active duty
- Date released from active duty
- Date of birth
- Date of death
- Date of burial (or scheduled burial date)
- Name and address of the cemetery or place of burial

### About the flag recipient

- Full name of the person who will receive the flag
- Relationship to the deceased Veteran
- Address and phone number

### Your information (if you are the applicant submitting on behalf of someone else)

- Your full name and address
- Your relationship to the deceased Veteran

### Documents to upload

- **DD Form 214** (Certificate of Release or Discharge from Active Duty) —
  this is the most common discharge document
- Other official military discharge documents are also accepted

**If you don't have the DD Form 214:** You can still submit this application.
Explain the situation in the Remarks section of the form. You can also request
the Veteran's discharge records from the NPRC:

- **Online:** [archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records](https://www.archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records)
- **Phone:** 1-86-NARA-NARA (1-866-272-6272)

NPRC can process urgent requests — such as those needed for burial — faster
than routine requests.

## How the application process works

{:.process-list}
1. **Fill out the application**
   Complete the online form. You can submit without signing in to VA.gov.
   If you sign in, we may be able to pre-fill some information from VA records.

2. **Upload documentation**
   Upload a copy of the Veteran's DD Form 214 or other discharge documents.
   If you don't have these documents, explain in the Remarks section.

3. **Submit your application**
   After you submit, you'll receive a confirmation number immediately on screen.
   We'll also send a confirmation email if you provide an email address.

4. **VA reviews your application**
   VA's National Cemetery Administration (NCA) reviews your application.
   For complete applications, VA's service standard is 2 business days.

5. **The flag is issued**
   The flag will be provided to the next-of-kin or authorized person listed on
   your application.

## Can I still use the paper form?

Yes. The paper version of VA Form 27-2008 is still available. You can pick it
up at:

- US Post Offices
- VA regional offices
- VA medical centers
- Most funeral homes

Mail the completed paper form to:

> NCA Field Programs Evidence Intake Center
> P.O. Box 5237
> Janesville, WI 53547

The online form is an alternative — not a replacement — for the paper process.
